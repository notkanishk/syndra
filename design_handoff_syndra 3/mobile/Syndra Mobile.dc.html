M00 · Motion and interaction

Four movements, and nothing else

Cohesion on touch is mostly motion discipline. Desktop Syndra has three timings; mobile adds one — a surface arriving from an edge — and forbids everything else. If a transition on any screen below is not one of these four, it is a bug.

press

90ms
Approve

Scale to .97 and back, on every tap target. The only feedback a finger gets, since there is no hover to inherit. Fires on touch-down, not on release.

settle

260ms

A row disclosing, a list reflowing, content arriving in place. Rows below are pushed, never overlapped — the operator keeps their place in the list.

rise

300ms

New on mobile. A sheet rises 10px from the edge it will live on. It is the only surface that travels — dialogs, menus and popovers all become this.

drain

per item
3 of 12 · 1 failed

Progress that reports as it goes, inline, never in a modal. Failures accumulate in the line rather than interrupting; the operator reads the outcome when it stops.

Forbidden

Swipe actions. Pull-to-refresh. Long-press menus. Parallax. Spring overshoot. Toasts. Skeleton shimmer that outlives the request. Every one of them hides a decision behind a gesture with no label, and the product's whole argument is that consequences are visible before you act.

Colour, unchanged

One violet per screen region. Lime is never a fill and never a button. Amber is a deadline or a broken assumption. Red is destructive only, and solid only once a rung-3 gesture is satisfied. Touch adds no colours — a phone screen has less room to spend them in, not more.

Targets and reach

44px minimum, 50px for anything destructive, 12px between a destructive control and a benign one with the benign one nearer the screen edge. Primary actions sit at the bottom inside the safe area. The top of the screen is for orientation, not for action.

Reduced motion

Each movement has a still form, not a shorter one. press becomes a one-frame tint. settle and rise cut straight to the final position. drain keeps its counting text and drops the bar's transition — the text was always the message.

M01 · Navigation

Item count decides the placement

Four or fewer destinations become a tab bar; more than four become the rail arriving as a sheet on rise. Order, labels and badges are the desktop rail's, unchanged — Advanced still appends, and nothing sits above the first four.

M02 · Header and freshness

One line the operator cannot scroll past

The freshness strip becomes a single line under the title with refresh as a 44px target. Where the read's age gates an action, the strip is sticky and stays while the list scrolls. No pull-to-refresh: a hidden gesture that silently re-reads a target undermines the one pattern every add-on screen depends on.

M03 · Signature component

The source capsule, and what replaces its popover

Three kinds, fixed vocabulary, dot before word — unchanged. What changes is the explanation: desktop hovers, touch discloses. The capsule stays in the row and the row's disclosure carries what the popover said.

M04 · Landing

Today, one block per screen-height

Every block is still something the operator can finish. The blocks stack in desktop order and keep their own actions — collapsing them into counts would make it the summary dashboard §04 refuses to be.

M05 · People

Search takes the whole screen

On the highest-traffic screen in the product, tapping search replaces the page: keyboard up, results under the field, nothing competing. Filters live in a sheet with a count on the trigger rather than a chip row that wraps to three lines.

M06 · E3 · one person's access

A1 here, because every row is a decision

Grouped by project, granted above automatic, every row carrying its source. Under about eight rows the labelled-line card wins: the operator is deciding rather than scanning, and the remove control belongs in view rather than behind a disclosure.

M07 · E2 · who can use this

Forty rows, so A2

Role → members. Every row still carries its source, and the count in the header is the answer to the question the operator asked. Disclosure carries who granted it and when it ends.

M08 · E1 · projects, roles, apps

A boundary, and the things inside it

A project owns roles; an app receives a token. They are not the same and never share a list. On mobile the two-pane index becomes one list that drills, with the project as the back-line.

M09 · E4 + E5 · giving access

The consequence, not the form

Bundles expand to roles and rules cascade further, so the preview is the screen — not a footnote under it. The sheet grows to fit the cascade and the confirm button stays pinned, so what you are about to cause is never scrolled out of sight from what you tap.

M10 · E6 · token debug

The most technical screen, and it still fits

“My app isn't seeing the roles it expects.” The answer is a comparison, so it stays a comparison: what Syndra holds, what the last token carried, and the sentence that explains the gap. Every id is a copy row.

M11 · E7 · both sides of a request

Rung 1, under the thumb

Approving is reversible, so it gets no ceremony — a pinned action bar and no dialog. Declining asks for a reason, because the member reads it. The member's side is the one screen that explains why an answer took time.

M12 · Every list view

Four states, no exceptions

Loading holds the row geometry so nothing jumps when data lands. Empty says what would appear here. Error names the endpoint. Degraded is the one that matters: when the backend has fallen back to demo data, every number on the screen is a lie and the banner says so before the list does.

M13 · S5 · access map

A neighbourhood becomes a list of neighbours

The desktop map draws one node and its immediate connections. At 390px a graph with labels is unreadable at any zoom, so the same idea is rendered as the thing it actually is: a centre, what reaches it, and what it reaches. Panning and pinching are not required to read it.

M14 · S6 + S7 · drift and reconcile

Two columns become two stanzas

Drift is a comparison — what Syndra intends against what the target reports — and a phone cannot hold two columns. The row becomes two lines with the difference stated in words, and reconciling is a plan the operator reads before applying.

M15 · S1 + S2b · bundles

Rung 2, with the gate in the label

Assigning a bundle to one person is Basic. Editing what a bundle contains changes access for everyone holding it, which is rung 2: a checkbox per consequence. On a phone the unchecked box may be scrolled out of view when the operator reaches the button, so the button states its own gate.

M16 · S2 + S3 + S4 · the causal chain

Three columns become one thread

A rule fires, a write queues, the write is recorded. Desktop reads it left to right; a phone reads it as one thread, and every screen in it carries a back-line naming where you came from and the cascade id you are following. The id stays a link everywhere it appears.

M17 · S8–S11 · the forensic floor

Read-mostly, and answering one question each

Four screens for when something has already happened. None is a dashboard. On a phone they are the easiest screens to get right and the easiest to ruin — the temptation is to add filters nobody asked for.

M18 · Add-on platform · navigation

Three rows in, one row out

The add-on platform adds a destination to Review, to System, and to the member's tabs — and removes nothing from Basic. On mobile that means the member's two tabs become three, and Advanced's sheet grows by two rows. The tab bar's four-item limit is why Storage is a member tab and not an operator one.

M19 · Member › storage

Three states, and the middle one is ordinary

A two-state page lies to the member in the middle. Access granted but not yet arrived is the common case, not an error, and it says so in the member's language. The connection strings are copy rows because a member is typing them into a file manager on the same phone.

M20 · Advanced › System › target

Four questions about one add-on

Is it answering, whose accounts are on it, what it can actually do, and what state somebody has left it in. Four tabs, in that order, with the freshness strip above them because it governs all four.

M21 · Review › withdrawn access

Two populations, never one count

Access somebody decided to take away that is still there. Merged into a single number it becomes a health score nobody acts on, so the two populations stay separate on a phone too: sessions that will end by themselves, and access that will not.

M22 · Plan, then apply

The plan is the thing you approve

Rehearse, read, apply — two steps in place, not a wizard. The rehearsal sheet goes full-height with a sticky footer carrying the plan's counts and its age, so the operator can scroll the whole plan without losing sight of what they are about to apply or how old it is.

M23 · Holds

One object, three faces

A hold takes away access a role still grants. The operator sees a hold, the member sees withheld access, and the review queue sees a list of them. Same record, three readings — and on a phone the word must never slip: the member's copy says withheld even when the operator's screen said hold.

M24 · Door cards

The one flow that is better on a phone

A card is enrolled by an operator standing next to the person, at the reader. Every other screen in this board is a desktop screen made to fit; this one belongs on a phone. Enrolment waits for a physical tap, and the screen says so in the present tense.

M25 · Dormant accounts

Bulk selection needs a mode, not a gesture

The one screen in the product built for bulk work. Long-press to select is invisible, so a named Select control in the header turns rows into checkboxes and raises a count bar. Rows that cannot be selected keep their dashed border and their stated reason.

M26 · The middle breakpoint

720–1080px, where the floor operator lives

Three states are enough to specify and few enough to test. At tablet width the rail returns as a 64px strip, tables regain three columns, sheets become dialogs again, and the bottom bar disappears. Nothing new is designed here — it is the desktop board with fewer columns.

Carried over unchanged

Every route, every screen name, every label. Advanced appends to Basic.

Open, before build

The dormant-account listing endpoint from §29 is still unbuilt; M25 assumes it returns the reason a row cannot be removed.
Card enrolment needs a reader-event stream for M24a's waiting state. Polling would work but the two-minute timeout must come from the server, not the client.
TrueNAS first, Unifi Access later — M18b's third member tab appears only once TrueNAS ships.
Provider latency in M17b is a measured round trip, not an uptime percentage. Confirm the backend reports it per read.
Reading order

Start at M00 — the four movements are the whole cohesion argument. Then M28, which is the first screen anyone sees, and M01, M02 and M32, which every other screen inherits. The rest can be read in any order against their desktop counterparts.

M28 · Sign-in on touch

The door, at 390px

One action: sign in through Zitadel. No email field, no password, no reset — Zitadel owns all of that, so there is nothing here to get wrong and nothing to rate-limit. The three states are told through the arch itself: resting, opening, and the door staying shut. Composition, colour and copy are the login handoff's; only the scale and the lit ring's behaviour change.

Touch has no pointer, so the orb's lit ring cannot track one. §5's keyboard fallback is the answer already in the spec: the ring locks to linear-gradient(0deg, #000 4%, transparent 80%) — lit from below, where the button is — at opacity 1 with the bloom at .9. The orb is lit by the thing you are about to press. Sessions are Zitadel sessions and last weeks, so most operators meet this screen once.

What touch changes

Arch 296 × 270, radius 131px. Wordmark 40px. Orb 40px at top 38.
The lit ring holds §5's static bottom-lit mask at opacity 1, bloom .9 — permanently, since there is no pointer to release it to.
Page background is #0a0b08, not the app shell's #080906.
Everything else — entrance timings, easings, copy, the three states — is the login handoff unchanged.

Height, not width

The composition does not compress. Below ~610px of viewport height the button's overhang collides with the Syn line, so a short window scrolls.
On a 390 × 844 phone there is room. On a landscape phone there is not — the stage keeps min-height: max(100vh, 800px) and scrolls.
The base text and both messages share bottom: 74px on desktop and bottom 30 here, so a state change never shifts the layout.

Reduced motion

Skips the entrance and the ambience. The ring keeps its static mask, which on touch it already has. Both state changes still happen — instantly if necessary, because the arch is what carries the meaning.

M32 · Back, history and deep links

Rules for the build

Tapping the active tab returns that tab to its root; it never reloads.
A sheet is a level of history — back closes it before leaving the screen.
Sign-out clears every stack. Sessions last weeks, so this is rare and deliberate.
Every screen is addressable, and every deep link lands on the screen itself, never on a parent with the child scrolled into view.
The system back gesture and the header's ‹ do the same thing, always.
