A dialog's three moments

A-22, G20 to G31. One sheet, three moments: before the press it says what will happen and asks; while pressing it forecasts in its own tense; after, it becomes its result and nothing it previewed is still on screen. The same shape for an act that takes something away and for one that only cancels something unsent. The title is the act in the button's words, the dismiss says what it keeps, and the colour follows what happens to the person, not the name of the button.

1 · Taking something away: Revoke, before · pressing · after

Avani Sood holds Community in Navidrome by hand, and it is in Zitadel. Before: the lede is the fact, the What-happens list is the loss, the before-line says how it travels, the pair is Keep it beside the solid red act. Pressing: the before-line's seat becomes the forecast and both buttons hold. After: the lede, the list and the before-line are gone; the sheet is its result, with one Close.

2 · Cancelling something unsent: the same sheet, quiet

The give never reached Zitadel, so nobody loses anything. The title changes with the act (Cancel the waiting give, no question mark), the body is one neutral line, the act is accent, and the dismiss is Keep it: two buttons never share a verb. The first frame is what staging shows today: a Revoke title over a cancel, two Cancels, and a red pre-act warning left standing above a green result.

3 · Button pairs (G24)

The act names its verb and its object. The dismiss is the ghost half of the pair and says what it keeps: Keep it (or them) when the act takes something away, retires, cancels or declines; Cancel only for a form that creates, asks or gives, where nothing exists yet to keep. After the result, one outline Close. Never OK, Confirm, Yes, or a second Cancel.

4 · What earns red, what earns amber, what stays quiet (G25)

Colour follows what the act does to a named person. Red is a loss that reaches a system: the What-happens list, the solid red confirm, the danger dot on a failure. Amber is a step left or a state to read before pressing: waits for you, a stale plan, an I-understand tick, a system not answering. Everything else is quiet text: what only records, asks, previews, or cancels something unsent.
